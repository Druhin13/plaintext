The examiners have raised a procedural point that needs to be settled before the next sitting. Under the current regulations a candidate who withdraws after the closing date but before the examination is recorded as absent, and an absence counts as an attempt. A candidate who withdraws before the closing date is not recorded at all. The difference matters because the regulations limit candidates to three attempts.

The point was raised because of a case last session in which a candidate withdrew four days after the deadline on medical grounds, was recorded as absent, and subsequently exhausted the attempt limit. The appeal committee found in the candidate's favour but did so on the specific facts rather than on any general principle, so the underlying ambiguity remains.

There are three ways to resolve it. The first is to keep the rule as it stands and rely on the appeal process to handle hard cases. This has the advantage of requiring no change and the disadvantage of pushing every marginal case through a committee that meets twice a year. The second is to create an explicit category of excused withdrawal, granted on documented grounds, which would not count as an attempt. This is what most comparable bodies do. The third is to abandon the attempt limit, which several members have argued for on other grounds and which would make the question moot.

My own preference is the second option. The attempt limit serves a purpose and I would not remove it casually, but the current rule punishes candidates for the timing of events they do not control. An excused withdrawal category can be defined narrowly enough that it does not become a routine escape. I would suggest limiting it to documented medical grounds and bereavement, with a single excused withdrawal per candidate.

The registrar has drafted wording and it is attached for comment.
