The prototype has been running in the test environment for six weeks and the results are mixed in an interesting way. Throughput is well above what we projected, largely because the batching layer turned out to be more effective than the design assumed. We had budgeted for four hundred operations per second and we are seeing close to eleven hundred under the same hardware allocation. That headroom is real and it changes what we can promise in the next release.

Latency is the problem. The median response time is acceptable at thirty-one milliseconds, but the distribution has a long tail that we do not yet understand. Roughly one request in four hundred takes more than two seconds, and a handful each day take more than ten. Those requests are not correlated with load, they are not correlated with payload size, and they are not clustered in time. The obvious explanations have been eliminated one by one over the last fortnight.

The current working hypothesis involves the connection pool. When a pooled connection has been idle beyond a threshold the driver revalidates it, and revalidation appears to block other work on the same thread. That would explain the absence of correlation with load, because idle connections are more likely when traffic is light. It does not obviously explain the ten-second cases, which are an order of magnitude beyond anything revalidation should cost.

We are instrumenting the driver directly rather than continuing to infer behaviour from application metrics. This requires a patched build and it will take about a week to get through review. In the meantime I would not make any commitment to a latency target in customer-facing documentation. Quoting a median is defensible; quoting a ninety-ninth percentile is not, until we know what is happening in the tail.

A secondary finding worth recording is that the retry logic amplifies the problem. A request that times out is retried twice, and the retries land on the same pool.
